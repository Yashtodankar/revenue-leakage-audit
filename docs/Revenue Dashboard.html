Where the money went

Of every R$100 of list-price revenue, about R$5 is left as profit

Product cost takes the largest share. Beyond that, freight (R$2.25M) and refunds (R$0.72M) each cost more than the whole profit.

Product cost is modelled at 70% of list price. List price is each product's median selling price. Freight is the real freight value in the data.

The three leaks

Refunds are the largest leak

Leakage of R$1.52M is more than twice total profit, and refunds are 47% of it. Without refunds, profit would be R$1.41M instead of R$0.69M.

Refunds are modelled: cancelled or unavailable orders, plus late deliveries rated 1–2 stars. 87% of refund value comes from the late-delivery rule.

Margin falls as freight takes a bigger share of the price

Lines where freight is over 20% of item price are 57% of lines but 27% of revenue, and every one of those bands loses money.

Margin by freight share of item price. Dashed line is the 15% target.

Discounted lines lose money above 10%

Margin is close to zero at 5–10% discount and negative above it. The 7.7% of lines above 10% discount lose roughly half of total profit.

Discount includes voucher-paid lines, which may be a payment method and not a price cut. Split vouchers before setting a cap. The last bar is cut off at −20%.

Cross-state orders earn about a third of the margin

Cross-state fulfilment is 69% of revenue. Its freight share is 18.7% against 13.2% for same-state orders, and leakage is 12.3% against 8.5%.

Olist has no sales-channel field, so route type stands in for channel.

Twelve states have a negative margin

São Paulo (SP) earns 68% of all profit. In the loss-making states freight is 20–27% of revenue, against 14% in SP.

What to do next

If late-delivery refunds are real

Fix delivery on long routes first. It is the largest modelled leak.

If freight over 20% of price holds in real data

Test minimum order values, freight surcharges or nearer-seller routing for low-priced items.

Before capping discounts

Separate vouchers from price cuts, then rerun the discount band analysis.

If rankings hold at 60% and 80% cost

Treat the state and category rankings as robust and act on the loss-making states.
